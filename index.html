Permits & fees

One backcountry permit covers the whole group for all three nights. Permits open a month before the first night and the AT shelters fill fast, so we book as soon as the headcount is final.

What we're booking

NightWhere we sleep
Sat, Oct 10Campsite #26, Dripping Spring Mountain
Sun, Oct 11Double Spring Gap Shelter
Mon, Oct 12Campsite #24, Rough Creek

How to book

  1. Open the Smokies backcountry permit page on Recreation.gov and sign in.
  2. Pick Saturday, October 10 as the start date and enter the group size.
  3. Add the three sites in order: #26, Double Spring Gap Shelter, then #24.
  4. Pay the fees, then print the permit and save a copy on your phone.
  5. Each driver buys a weekly parking tag on Recreation.gov, prints it, and leaves it face-up on the passenger side of the dashboard. The tag has to list that car's license plate.

Questions or problems booking: Backcountry Office, 865-436-1297, 8 am to 5 pm.

Park rules to know

  • No more than 8 people in a party.
  • Because we booked the shelter, we have to sleep inside it on night 2. No tents there.
  • Hang all food, trash, and anything with a scent on the bear cables every night.
  • Fires only in the existing fire rings, using wood that's already dead on the ground.
  • Pack out everything we bring in, including trash and used toilet paper.

Food & water

Everyone packs their own food and shares stoves in groups of two or three. Plan on about 1.5–2 pounds of food per day, roughly 2,500–3,500 calories.

Meals to pack

MonPack itPack itPack it
TuePack itIn GatlinburgHome

That's 3 breakfasts, 2 trail lunches and 3 dinners, plus snacks for all four days. Three or four snacks a day is about right.

Cooking and bears

  • Cook and eat in the campsite's cooking area by the fire ring, never in or near tents.
  • After dinner, everything with a scent goes on the bear cables: food, trash, toothpaste, lip balm, sunscreen.
  • Pack out all leftovers and trash. Don't bury or burn food.

Water, day by day

Day 1
Jakes Creek on the way up, and a spring at Campsite #26. Backup sources are marked on the map in case the spring is dry.
Day 2
Fill every bottle before leaving camp, because Miry Ridge is dry. There's a marked source near Silers Bald and springs at the shelter, and the North Carolina–side spring is the more reliable one.
Day 3
Goshen Prong and the Little River run alongside the trail, so water is easy.
Day 4
Along the Little River the whole way out.

Filter or treat all water, even when it looks clean. Carry at least 3 liters of capacity.

The route

Jakes Creek, Miry Ridge & Appalachian Trail Loop

Starts and ends at Elkmont, near Gatlinburg, TN

Length
26.1 mi
Elevation gain
4,852 ft
Nights
3
Moving time
13–14 hr

The right size for us: four days, nothing longer than 8.7 miles, and the climbing is all in the first half. We go up Jakes Creek to Jakes Gap and follow Miry Ridge up to the Appalachian Trail. Day two walks the North Carolina–Tennessee line over Silers Bald to a shelter at Double Spring Gap.

From there it's almost all downhill. Goshen Prong drops about 3,000 feet past cascades to the Little River, and the last morning is an easy old logging-railroad grade back to the car, past Huskey Branch Falls. We get three different camps along the way: a ridge-top campsite, a shelter on the crest, and a spot by the river.

Day by day

  1. Day 1

    Elkmont to Campsite #26, Dripping Spring Mountain

    5.4 mi+2,490 ft−141 ft3–3.5 hr

    A steady climb from the parking lot: 3.3 miles up Jakes Creek Trail to Jakes Gap, then 1.9 miles on Miry Ridge to the camp spur. The camp has a spring. If the spout is dry, there are backup sources marked on the map.

  2. Day 2

    Campsite #26 to Double Spring Gap Shelter

    7.5 mi+1,929 ft−938 ft4–4.5 hr

    Finish Miry Ridge over Jenkins Knob, then head east on the AT over Silers Bald. Fill every bottle before leaving camp, because Miry Ridge has no water. There's a marked source near Silers Bald. The shelter sits at 5,507 ft, sleeps 12, and has springs on both the NC and TN sides. The NC spring is the more reliable one.

  3. Day 3

    Double Spring Gap to Campsite #24, Rough Creek

    8.7 mi+400 ft−3,058 ft4.5–5 hr

    A short 0.6-mile climb east on the AT to the Goshen Prong junction, then a long descent beside cascades past Campsite #23 (Camp Rock) down to the Little River. Turn upstream for the last stretch to camp. It's the longest day, but it's mostly downhill, so it's a good day for trekking poles.

  4. Day 4

    Campsite #24 back to Elkmont

    4.5 mi+33 ft−682 ft1.5–2 hr

    Out along the Little River Trail, wide and gentle the whole way, past Huskey Branch Falls. We should be at the cars by lunch.

Bar length shows each day's miles. The longest day is 8.7 mi.

Why it works

  • A good challenge for the group without any brutal days
  • Three different campsites: ridge, AT shelter, river
  • Plenty of water in most sections
  • Two climbing days, then two descending days, so the hard part is done early
  • Great scenery: the AT crest, Silers Bald, Goshen Prong's cascades, Huskey Branch Falls
  • High-country color turns first, so early October could be peak
  • A roof for the night at the shelter, with no tents to pitch

Watch out for

  • Day 1 is a 2,490 ft climb straight from the car with full packs
  • Miry Ridge is dry, and springs run low in a dry fall. Carry extra on Day 2.
  • The crest runs 10–20°F colder than Elkmont. Plan for nights near freezing by late October.
  • With a shelter reservation we have to sleep in the shelter (no tents), and we'll share it with AT hikers
  • Three nights means more food to carry and more time off
  • Few ways out once we're up on the crest

Rabbit Creek & Abrams Creek Loop

Starts and ends at Abrams Creek Ranger Station, off Happy Valley Road

Length
15.1 mi
Elevation gain
2,805 ft
Nights
2
Moving time
7.5–8 hr

A shorter loop in the quiet west corner of the park. Rabbit Creek Trail climbs from Abrams Creek over Pine Mountain to Cades Cove. The return follows Abrams Creek past Abrams Falls, which is only 20 feet tall but carries a huge volume of water, to Little Bottoms, a favorite campsite right on the creek.

It's lower and warmer than Plan A, and there's water nearly everywhere. The catch is the shape: two short days around one long one. Day 2 does pass the Abrams Falls Trailhead about halfway, which gives us a pit toilet and a road if anyone needs to bail.

Day by day

  1. Day 1

    Abrams Creek Ranger Station to Campsite #16, Scott Gap

    2.5 mi+413 ft−299 ft1–1.5 hr

    An easy start up Rabbit Creek Trail, which works well if we drive in the same morning. Cross Abrams Creek near the ranger station and climb gently to Scott Gap, where Hannah Mountain Trail comes in.

  2. Day 2

    Campsite #16 to Campsite #17, Little Bottoms

    9.1 mi+1,749 ft−1,522 ft4.5–5 hr

    The big one. Continue on rocky Rabbit Creek Trail past Campsite #15 and over Pine Mountain, which has views and the only cell signal on the route, then take a steep descent to the Abrams Falls Trailhead in Cades Cove. That's the pit toilets and a good lunch stop. Then head west along Abrams Creek past the falls to Little Bottoms.

  3. Day 3

    Campsite #17 back to Abrams Creek

    3.5 mi+643 ft−978 ft2–2.5 hr

    Little Bottoms Trail and Cooper Road back to Abrams Creek Campground, a short walk from the ranger station parking.

Bar length shows each day's miles. The longest day is 8.7 mi.

Why it works

  • Shorter: 15.1 miles and two nights, so less time off and lighter food bags
  • Great on water, following creeks most of the way
  • Pit toilets at the Abrams Falls Trailhead, halfway through Day 2
  • Stays under 2,500 ft, so nights are warmer than on the crest
  • Abrams Falls, plus Little Bottoms as the last camp
  • An easy exit at Cades Cove if someone gets hurt or sick

Watch out for

  • Lopsided: Day 2 is 9.1 miles between two short days
  • Three creek crossings that can mean wet feet after rain. Bring camp shoes.
  • Abrams Falls Trail is one of the busiest in the park around midday
  • No swimming at Abrams Falls. The current is strong, and people have drowned there.
  • Cades Cove has lots of bears, so use the food cables every night
  • No ridgeline or AT views, and low-elevation color peaks later in the season
